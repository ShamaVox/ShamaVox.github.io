The rule

If your AI feature works in a demo but fails on real customer inputs, start by measuring the failure, not by adding another agent.

QualityHow often is the output correct on real customer inputs?
TraceabilityWhich field, step, prompt, or model caused the failure?
ControlWhat can be repaired automatically, and what needs a human?
Release safetyDid the latest change make the system worse?

Production proof

A multi-agent platform that put retailers' products on sale the same day.

As cofounder and CTO of an AI-native wholesale platform for boutique and enterprise retailers, I designed and built the system and took it from proof of concept to paying customers.

TimelineProof of concept to MVP in four months, then paying customers.
SystemPython, FastAPI, Next.js, Postgres, pgvector, Redis, AWS, Claude and OpenAI APIs, Datadog.
BusinessPurchase orders turned into live, sellable store listings with sizing and color sync.

Supplier catalogs arrived in every format imaginable. Colors were abbreviated, size runs were written five different ways, and product images came in every size and resolution. Retailers waited days for listings while inventory sat on the shelf.

After launch, listings were created the moment a purchase order arrived, and retailers were selling within an hour to a day. Revenue rose about 20% per store, and retailers saw their best sellers while stock was still fresh.

Getting a model to read a catalog was the easy part. The hard part was making every field correct on data nobody had seen before. So I built the checking before the features: a golden set, field-level evals, confidence scoring, and prompt and model versions logged with every output. Specialized agents for extraction, sizing, quantity and color were coordinated by deterministic code, and models were chosen by golden-set score, not by demo.
